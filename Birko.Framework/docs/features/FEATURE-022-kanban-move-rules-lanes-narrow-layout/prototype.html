PROTOTYPE — FEATURE-022, a mockup of the proposed b-kanban behaviour. Not wired to real data; not the real component.

Kanban board: move rules, lanes, a narrow layout, and buttons on cards

What we're asking you

This board models DraCode's project Board on the proposed component. People may only re-prioritise cards in To do; agents move everything else.

  1. Drag a To-do card between the priority lanes. Try dragging it into In progress or Done, and try dragging a card out of a locked column.
  2. Turn on Make the next save fail, then move a card between lanes: watch it go back with an error.
  3. Click Retry on the failed card: it must not pick the card up or count as clicking the card.
  4. With the keyboard: Tab to a card, Space to pick it up, ↑/↓ between lanes, ←/→ between columns, Enter to drop, Esc to cancel.
  5. Drag the width slider down with Stack when narrow on, then off. Compare the two ways lanes look when stacked.

The answer wanted: whether each behaviour is right (the questions at the bottom, one per decision). Not being judged: colours, fonts, wording and the card's content — those come from the real component and from DraCode.

The board's own width is set by the slider (as a phone, or the space beside a docked chat, would set it).
Press Space on a card to pick it up. Up and Down move between lanes, Left and Right between columns. Enter drops, Escape cancels.

What the board tells the app

Questions — one per proposed decision

D1 In progress, Blocked·Failed and Done are locked: no card goes in or out by mouse or keyboard. Is the striped look plus "locked" enough to tell a person why a drop is refused?
D2 DraCode's own move rule here: "only between To-do lanes". Locked columns are checked first. Anything a lock plus a rule can't express for you?
D3 A move can be refused as it happens (try a locked column): the card never leaves. Right?
D4 A failed save puts the card back with an error on it, and the log shows "reverted", not a second move. Right? Should the card also offer an undo after a successful move, or is that the app's choice?
D5 Lanes stack inside To do, each with a label, a count and its own drop area. Should an empty lane stay visible (as here) or collapse?
D6 Stacking is opt-in and follows the board's own width. Compare "grouped" and "one list" for lanes when stacked: which should the real component do? (Open in idea.md's fog list.)
D7 To do has reordering off: a card dropped into a lane takes the app's order (here: oldest first), and there is no drop line between cards. Right?
D8 Retry, a link or a field on a card never starts a drag or counts as a card click. Right?
D9 Keyboard: ↑/↓ crosses lanes, ←/→ crosses columns, locked columns are skipped and announced. Does it feel right?
D10 Was this mockup enough to decide, or is something missing?
